# Level 0 Mindmap — Market Reading Foundations

## CENTER NODE

# Level 0 — Market Reading Foundations

**Core purpose:**
Train learners to read markets from facts, not feelings.

**Level 0 teaches learners to:**
Observe price clearly → understand market mechanics → read candles as records → respect timeframe weight → map structure → audit evidence → classify condition → locate price → apply asset-specific checks → practice with discipline → write useful notes → complete a full foundation mission.

**Level 0 does not teach:**
Buy/sell signals, guaranteed profit systems, shortcut strategies, live execution models, or advanced risk management.

---

# Stage 1 — Orientation and Market Mechanics

## Module 0.1 — What Lurnava Teaches: Reading, Practice, Review

**Main idea:**
The learner is not here for tips or signals. They are here to build a repeatable training process.

**Mindmap branches:**

* Stop chasing tips
  * No signal dependency
  * No random content consumption
  * No hype-based confidence
* Understand the Lurnava loop
  * Study
  * Practice
  * Mission
  * Review
* Separate activity from training
  * Watching is not training
  * Clicking is not training
  * Reviewable practice is training
* Learn the first market differences
  * Forex = relationship between currencies
  * Gold = speed and volatility need context
  * Crypto = hype, leverage, and venue mechanics can distort movement

**Learner outcome:**
The learner understands how Lurnava trains them and why structured practice matters.

---

## Module 0.2 — What a Market Really Is

**Main idea:**
A market is not magic. Price moves because orders meet, liquidity changes, and transactions happen.

**Mindmap branches:**

* Market mechanics
  * Buyers
  * Sellers
  * Orders
  * Transactions
* Bid, ask, and spread
  * Best buyer
  * Best seller
  * Gap between them
* Liquidity
  * Deep liquidity = smoother movement
  * Thin liquidity = jumpier movement
* Asset-specific mechanics
  * Forex = quote network
  * Gold = layered market structure
  * Crypto = fragmented venues and liquidity pools

**Learner outcome:**
The learner can explain price movement as market interaction, not random chart behavior.

# Stage 2 — Chart Records and Timeframe Weight

## Module 0.3 — Candles Are Records, Not Signals

**Main idea:**
Candles record what happened. They do not give instructions.

**Mindmap branches:**

* Candle anatomy
  * Open
  * High
  * Low
  * Close
  * Body
  * Wick
* Record thinking
  * What happened?
  * Where did price reject?
  * Where did price close?
* Signal trap
  * One candle is not enough
  * Candle shape needs context
* Asset-specific candle behavior
  * Forex candles = quote records
  * Gold candles = volatility records
  * Crypto candles = venue records

**Learner outcome:**
The learner stops treating candles as signals and starts reading them as records.

---

## Module 0.4 — The Hierarchy of Evidence

**Main idea:**
Not all chart evidence has equal weight. Bigger context matters before smaller detail.

**Mindmap branches:**

* Timeframe hierarchy
  * Higher timeframe = broader map
  * Lower timeframe = local detail
* Evidence weight
  * Bigger structure carries more context
  * Smaller movement can be noise
* Narrative vs detail
  * Broad story
  * Local movement
* Timeframe conflict
  * Lower timeframe strength inside higher-timeframe weakness
  * Strong local candle near bigger resistance
* Top-down habit
  * Broad map first
  * Local detail second

**Learner outcome:**
The learner can prioritize higher-timeframe context before trusting lower-timeframe movement.

**Curriculum caution:**
This module should stay focused on timeframe weight. Full evidence-audit workflow belongs mainly to Module 0.6.

## Module 0.11 — Crypto Reading Lab: Venue Fragmentation and Liquidity Spikes

A crypto move needs venue proof before it becomes a cleaner reading.

**Mindmap branches:**

  * Wider market confirms or does not confirm
* CEX vs DEX
  * Order book behavior
  * Pool behavior
* DEX price impact
  * Thin pool
  * Loud wick
  * Local distortion
* Venue disagreement
  * CEX, DEX, aggregate feed do not match
* Venue gap behavior
  * Gap closes
  * Gap spreads
  * Spike fades
* BTC background
  * Helpful context, not the full answer
* Cross-venue verification

  * Did others confirm?
  * Did price hold?

**Learner outcome:**
The learner can judge whether a crypto move is broad market movement or local venue distortion.

* Open interest

  * Position unwind
  * Needs price context
* Price + OI combinations
  * Price up + OI up
  * Price up + OI down
  * Price down + OI up
  * Price down + OI down
* Funding pressure
  * Crowded longs
  * Crowded shorts
  * Pressure clue, not signal
* Crowded positioning
  * Fragile pressure
  * Forced exits
* Liquidation flushes
  * Fast forced movement
  * Not automatic reversal
* Squeezes
  * Long squeeze
  * Short squeeze
* Post-flush settlement
  * Stabilize
  * Continue
  * Chop
  * Fail

**Learner outcome:**
The learner can read derivatives pressure without treating funding, OI, or liquidation as direct trade signals.

# Stage 5 — Practice Discipline and Review

## Module 0.13 — Practice Discipline Lab: Simulation Mindset and Process Quality

**Main idea:**
Practice is not random clicking. Good practice needs a valid process.

**Mindmap branches:**

* Simulator mindset
  * Training device, not video game
* Valid practice attempt
  * Reason before action
  * Review condition before result
* Outcome bias
  * Good result does not prove good process
  * Bad result does not prove bad process
* Active idle
  * Standing aside is valid practice
* Boredom clicking
  * Acting because nothing is happening
* Chasing movement
  * Acting because price moved fast
* Reading vs execution
  * Reading quality first
  * Execution comes later
* Replay protocol
  * Review process, not outcome revenge

**Learner outcome:**
The learner can use simulation practice without turning it into guessing or fake confidence.
